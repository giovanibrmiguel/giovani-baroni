Giovani Baroni spent over a decade in São Paulo's independent scene, most of it fronting the alt-rock band [sukya || porno](/sukya). After moving to Mexico City, he started over: new language, new instruments, new process. Instead of chasing singles, he's spent the years since writing dense, long-form songs — unpredictable structures, emotional directness, no interest in a quick hook. He calls it "music by nerds, for nerds": progressive rock's complexity next to the rawness of Brazilian indie.

The move to CDMX meant leaving old projects behind and rebuilding from scratch. What replaced them is a network of collaborators spread from Brazil to Berlin, which is part of why the songs move between Portuguese, English, and Spanish. The music draws on cinema, literature, and ocean imagery — aquatic synths, phasers, and heavy delay standing in for the feeling of internal change.

That work comes together on his debut conceptual album, [bLU](/blu), arriving September 2026: a ten-track journey built to be heard front to back.
